# Executive story architecture

Load when planning a new deck, rewriting a deck's storyline, or reviewing the Executive Summary and action titles.

## Decision brief (internal planning, not a mandatory slide)

Capture in a few lines:

- Audience and decision authority: who will act on the recommendations?
- Main business question: what needs to change, continue, stop, or be investigated?
- Stakes and options: risks, opportunity, constraints, possible alternatives.
- Evidence: confirmed inputs, freshness, gaps, authorized source locations.
- Decision ask: approve, reprioritize, allocate, initiate a pilot, maintain course, or request targeted validation.
- Communication format: executive pre-read (self-contained), live presentation (less body text), or working session.

Do not make up ownership, budget, implementation timing or causal mechanisms.

## Default deck order

1. Optional mandated cover/title slide.
2. **Executive Summary**: one governing message, typically up to three independent insights and one to three prioritized, defensible recommendations. Where relevant, explicit decision requested / options / risks / confidence. A concise summary can span two slides when truly necessary, rather than shrinking text to fit.
3. Evidence sequence supporting the summary, organized by business driver, priority or decision. A context slide belongs here only if it materially changes the evaluation.
4. Business implications and recommendations, including alternatives or constraints where useful.
5. Conclusion / decision / next steps, explicitly linked back to the summary. A short executive deck may combine steps 4 and 5.
6. Clearly labeled appendix with deep dives, alternative segmentation, derivations, data tables, sensitivities, methods and source details. The appendix is navigable and not part of the primary story.

Do not front-load methodology, source history or a dashboard tour. A client-mandated section order outranks this default; explain any material trade-off.

## Executive Summary pattern

Use short standalone statements, not isolated KPI callouts:

- **Top line**: Governing answer to the business question, grounded in actual findings.
- **Insight 1-3**: Observed behavior + meaningful comparator + business consequence; explicitly mark unclear causes.
- **Recommendations**: Prioritized action + why + expected direction of impact (quantify only when validated) + risk/uncertainty + any known decision required.
- **Decision ask**: If appropriate, name the approval or choice leadership must make; if unknown, say "Decision to be defined" only in draft, not as a fabricated final commitment.

Do not duplicate the same executive summary paragraph on subsequent slides. Treat it as an executive map; details and supporting evidence belong later.

## Action-title grammar

A good action title is a complete, defensible sentence with a business point:

`[What changed or matters] + [relative to what] + [business consequence, if justified]`

OR

`[Recommended action] + [evidence-backed rationale]`

Poor: `Customer performance`; `Channel comparison`; `Executive findings`.

Better (illustrative wording only; not real data):

- `Repeat purchases lag the prior-year pace despite stronger acquisition`
- `Retention underperforms plan in two customer segments; prioritize a targeted diagnostic`
- `The mix shift explains part of the revenue decline; confirm the margin impact before reallocating spend`

Avoid expressing unverified causality: "Channel mix changed as retention fell" is observation; "Channel mix caused retention to fall" requires separate causal evidence.

If there is not enough evidence for a takeaway, use a transparent diagnostic question (e.g. "Is the reported improvement robust to return adjustments?") and flag it as pending, not a confirmed conclusion.

## Ghost-deck / horizontal logic

Read **only the titles of the content slides**. They should answer, in a coherent sequence:

1. What is the management-level answer?
2. What is the most important change, problem or opportunity relative to a baseline?
3. What supporting arguments and constraints establish its importance?
4. What should leadership do, and why now (if urgency is established)?

Use reverse storyboarding on existing decks: extract current titles, write the single message of each slide, re-order or retitle only with authorization. Remove redundant slides; relocate tangential evidence to appendix rather than merely hiding it.

## Vertical logic / slide test

For every slide, write five working labels before drawing:

1. Claim (action title)
2. Evidence (chart/table)
3. What the evidence does *not* prove (limitation)
4. Implication or action (why the audience cares)
5. Scope statement (footer)

The evidence must support the claim. If a side paragraph only paraphrases visible data, replace it with an interpretation, hypothesis with caveat, decision consequence or follow-up action.

## Recommendations taxonomy

- **Act**: sufficient evidence for a bounded commercial/operational action.
- **Validate**: high-value opportunity or concern, but data/causality needs confirmation.
- **Monitor**: no current intervention is justified; specify the indicator or trigger if known.
- **Maintain**: evidence supports continuing an approach; explain what would change the decision.

When none can be justified, be explicit about the evidence gap rather than inventing actions. A recommendation to validate is itself a legitimate decision if it is specific and proportionate.
